LONG PAINT BRUSHSTROKES CREATE THE SHAPES. Draw the figure and action from
long continuous curved or diagonal brush gestures. One curved vermilion
stroke builds a shoulder; long dark sweeps build hair and clothing; a few
overlapping curved strokes build a sleeve. An immense creamy-ivory stroke
over red builds fire when it is part of the brief. Each stroke has a broad
connected body, an irregular edge and a tapered or split end. Shapes follow
the brush's continuous movement. Keep stroke interiors relatively quiet;
the energy lives in their large silhouettes and directional overlap.
Use raw bristle endings and gaps of muted violet underpainting.

The face is a shadow wedge with two or three accents. Clothing is one or
two broad masses; equipment is a simple painted shape. Leave contours open
and merge parts of the figure into the brush gesture. A large close subject,
forceful diagonal action, strong overlap and two distant ruin silhouettes
give cinematic depth. Broad warm light against quiet violet shadows.
